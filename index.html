    The complete mortgage guide

    How a fixed-rate mortgage works, what drives your payment, and how to pay less over time.

    The fixed-rate mortgage formula

    A standard fixed-rate loan uses the same payment every month. That payment is calculated so the balance reaches exactly zero at the end of the term:

    M = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1)

    M is the monthly principal-and-interest payment, P is the loan amount, r is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12) and n is the number of payments. A 30-year loan has 360 payments. If the rate is exactly 0%, the formula simplifies to P ÷ n, which this calculator handles automatically.

    Small changes in rate matter more than most buyers expect. On a $320,000 loan over 30 years, moving from 6% to 7% adds roughly $2,000 to the monthly cost of the loan across a year and more than $140,000 in interest over its life. Always compare offers using total interest, not just the monthly figure.

    What PITI means for affordability

    Lenders talk about PITI: principal, interest, taxes and insurance. Principal reduces what you owe. Interest is the lender’s charge for borrowing. Property taxes are set by your local government, usually as a percentage of assessed value. Homeowners insurance protects the structure and is required by almost every lender.

    Many borrowers pay taxes and insurance through an escrow account. The lender collects one-twelfth of the annual bill each month and pays it on your behalf. That is why your payment can change even on a fixed-rate loan: if taxes or insurance premiums rise, your escrow amount rises with them.

    HOA or condo fees are not part of PITI, but they are part of what you pay to live in the home. Lenders include them when calculating your debt-to-income ratio, so a high HOA fee can reduce the loan amount you qualify for. Add maintenance too. A common planning rule is to set aside one to two percent of the home’s value each year for repairs.

    How amortization shifts from interest to principal

    Early in the loan, most of each payment is interest because interest is charged on the full balance. On a 30-year loan at 6.5%, the first payment on a $320,000 loan is about 83% interest. Each month the balance falls slightly, so the interest portion shrinks and the principal portion grows. By the final years, nearly the whole payment goes to principal.

    This is why the amortization schedule above matters. It shows exactly how much equity you build each year, and it explains why selling or refinancing in the first few years often leaves you with little principal paid down.

    15-year vs. 30-year terms

    A 15-year loan carries a higher monthly payment, but it usually has a lower rate and builds equity much faster. On the same $320,000 loan, a 15-year term at 5.75% costs roughly $2,657 per month and about $158,000 in total interest. A 30-year term at 6.5% costs about $2,023 per month and about $408,000 in interest. The shorter loan saves about $250,000, but it demands about $634 more every month.

    If the higher payment strains your budget, a 30-year loan with extra principal payments is a flexible middle path. You keep the lower required payment and can pay down faster when cash allows.

    Extra payments and bi-weekly schedules

    Every extra dollar sent to principal stops accruing interest for the rest of the loan. An extra $200 per month on a 30-year, $320,000 loan at 6.5% can shorten the loan by roughly five years and save more than $80,000 in interest. Annual lump sums from a bonus or tax refund work the same way.

    Bi-weekly plans pay half the monthly amount every two weeks. That produces 26 half-payments, or 13 full payments, per year. The one extra payment goes to principal. Check that your servicer applies extra funds to principal immediately and does not charge fees for the program. You can often get the same effect by adding one-twelfth of your payment to each monthly payment.

    PMI and the 20% equity milestone

    Private mortgage insurance protects the lender if you put down less than 20%. It typically costs between 0.3% and 1.5% of the loan amount per year, depending on credit score and down payment. On a conventional loan you can usually ask to cancel PMI once your balance reaches 80% of the original home value. It must end automatically when the balance reaches 78%, provided you are current on payments.

    Government-backed loans work differently. FHA loans carry their own mortgage insurance premiums, which often last for the life of the loan when the down payment is under 10%. VA loans have no monthly mortgage insurance but charge a funding fee. Compare these rules, not only the rate.

    What lenders evaluate

    Lenders judge risk using several factors. Your credit score sets your rate tier. Your debt-to-income ratio compares monthly debts, including the new housing payment, with gross monthly income; many lenders prefer a total ratio below 43% to 45%. They verify employment history and income stability, require documentation of assets and reserves, and order an appraisal to confirm the home is worth the price.

    Your down payment affects loan-to-value ratio (LTV), which is the loan divided by the home’s value. Lower LTV means lower risk and often a lower rate. To improve your terms, pay down revolving debt, avoid new credit before closing, and shop at least three lenders within a short window so the credit inquiries count as one.

    Fixed vs. adjustable rates

    A fixed-rate loan holds the same rate for the whole term. An adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM), such as a 5/6 ARM, holds a fixed rate for the first five years and then adjusts every six months based on an index. ARMs start lower but carry rate-reset risk. They suit buyers who expect to move or refinance before the reset. This calculator models fixed-rate loans.

    Using this calculator well

    Start with realistic taxes and insurance for your area, not national averages. Test a range of rates, since quotes change daily. Save two or three scenarios and compare total interest, not only the monthly payment. Finally, treat the result as an estimate. Your lender’s Loan Estimate is the document that matters.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much should I put down on a house?

    Many lenders accept 3% to 5% down, but 20% avoids PMI and lowers your monthly payment. Balance a larger down payment against keeping an emergency fund after closing.

    How do I remove PMI?

    On conventional loans you can request removal once your balance reaches 80% of the original value. It ends automatically at 78%. Some lenders also allow removal based on a new appraisal.

    Fixed or adjustable rate: which is better?

    Fixed rates give payment certainty. Adjustable rates start lower but can rise after the intro period. If you plan to stay long term, fixed is usually safer.

    Do extra payments really save money?

    Yes. Extra principal lowers the balance that interest is charged on, so you pay less interest and finish sooner. Use the payoff accelerator to see the exact effect.

    How do property taxes and escrow work?

    Your lender collects one-twelfth of your annual tax and insurance each month, holds it in escrow and pays the bills for you. Your payment can change when those bills change.

    What is LTV and why does it matter?

    Loan-to-value is your loan divided by the home’s value. Lower LTV means lower lender risk, better rates and no PMI at 80% or below.

    Is a bi-weekly payment plan worth it?

    It works because it adds one extra payment per year. You can get the same result by adding one-twelfth of your payment each month, without third-party fees.

    Does the calculator include closing costs?

    No. Closing costs commonly run 2% to 5% of the loan and are paid separately. Your lender’s Loan Estimate lists them.

    How accurate are the results?

    They are estimates based on the numbers you enter. Actual payments depend on your lender, escrow adjustments and any fees. Confirm with a Loan Estimate.

    How are calculations handled in the browser?

    All math runs locally in JavaScript on your device. Nothing you enter is uploaded. Saved scenarios are kept in your browser’s local storage and can be cleared at any time.
